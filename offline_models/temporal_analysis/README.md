Option 2: Distilling Temporal Dynamics ($\Delta W$, Gradient Signatures & Loss Curves)In this setup, your distiller network ingests time-series sequences of parameter movements and scalar responses over the last $K$ steps: weight delta magnitudes $\Vert{}\Delta W_t\Vert{}$, cosine similarities between consecutive updates, finite-difference gradient probes, and loss deltas $\Delta \mathcal{L}_t$.StrengthsArchitecture-Agnostic ("Free" Generalization): This is the biggest leverage point. A trajectory of parameter vectors and loss dynamics looks mathematically identical whether it comes from an MLP, a Transformer LoRA adapter, or a physics simulator. Vectors moving across an unobserved loss surface follow universal optimization dynamics:Oscillation across narrow ravines (alternating sign flips with stagnant loss).Vanishing steps (magnitudes collapsing toward zero).Plateau drifting vs. sharp-basin overshooting.Extremely Compact Input Dimension: You are not feeding millions of forward-pass activations. You are feeding a compact multi-variate time-series tensor (e.g., $[K \text{ steps} \times D_{\text{telemetry}}]$ where $D$ is simply norm, alignment, step size, loss, and probe agreements). A lightweight 1D-CNN or small GRU/Transformer can ingest this effortlessly.Direct Alignment with LLM Meta-Optimization: The meta-learning LLM’s job is fundamentally that of an adaptive controller. It needs to know: Are we oscillating? Did that jump overshoot? Is momentum carrying us the wrong way?Bottlenecks & PitfallsBlack-Box Blindness: While temporal data tells the LLM how the search is moving, it cannot tell the LLM what the network is actually computing. It diagnoses optimization pathology (e.g., "Step size is too aggressive across coordinate 2"), but lacks semantic awareness of the underlying functional mapping.The Verdict: Bang-for-Buck WinnerOption 2 (Temporal Behavior & Optimization Dynamics) wins by a wide margin for initial implementation and scaling.
